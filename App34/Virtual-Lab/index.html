Concept & Vocabulary

How astronomers read starlight

The Core Idea

When white light passes through a prism, it spreads into a continuous rainbow — a continuous spectrum. A star's surface produces light this way too. But starlight must first pass through the star's cooler outer atmosphere, where atoms of different elements absorb very specific wavelengths of light, each one tuned to the exact energy needed to excite an electron. This carves thin dark gaps — absorption lines — into the rainbow at wavelengths unique to each element, like a fingerprint. By measuring where those gaps fall, astronomers identify which elements are present, and by measuring how strong the gaps are, they can even estimate the star's surface temperature.
